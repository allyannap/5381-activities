County-Level ICE Public-Information Report

ICE detention patterns are concentrated primarily in Southern states, with California, Texas, Florida, and Arizona accounting for the majority of reported facilities. The data shows a steady increase in facility counts over time, indicating growing demand or capacity constraints at these centers.

The Latino demographic group is disproportionately represented among detainees, with 63% of all detainees being non-US citizens. This trend suggests that immigration enforcement efforts may be targeting this population more heavily than others.

Women and children are also a significant portion of the detained population, particularly in states like Florida and Arizona where women make up a substantial percentage of detainees. The data indicates that these groups face unique challenges and vulnerabilities during their time at ICE facilities.

While there is no clear trend or pattern over time, some observations can be made:

1. States with higher percentages of Latino residents (e.g., California) tend to have higher facility counts, suggesting potential resource allocation disparities.
2. The growth in facility counts may indicate increasing pressure on ICE facilities
