ICE detention patterns are concentrated primarily in Southern states, with the highest counts and rates observed in Texas. The data shows that Latino individuals, regardless of citizenship status, have been detained at a higher rate than non-Latino individuals across all facilities. Notably, the Adelanto ICE Processing Center in Los Angeles County has seen significant increases in detention counts over time, particularly for Latino detainees.

The data also reveals that women from Caribbean and Latin American backgrounds are disproportionately represented among detainees at the Broward Transitional Center in Florida. Furthermore, the Eloy Detention Center in Arizona has experienced a notable increase in non-citizen detainees, with both men (1892) and women (684) being detained.

Observable changes over time include an increase in detention counts for Latino individuals across all facilities, while rates of detention appear to be relatively stable. However, the data does not provide information on trends or patterns specific to individual facilities or states.
